Title:

NDC Calculator Fails to Handle Liquid Medications (mg → mL Conversion, Total Volume, Concentration Parsing)

Summary:

Our current NDC Calculator correctly handles solid dosage forms (tablets/capsules) but fails for all liquid medications, especially pediatric antibiotics such as amoxicillin, where prescriptions are written in mg, but products are dispensed in mL.
Because the system lacks support for concentration parsing and mg→mL conversions, it produces incorrect total quantities, incorrect package selection, and missing safety warnings.

This results in inaccurate calculations and makes the tool unsafe and unusable for liquid medications.

🔥 Problem Description (What’s Broken)

Liquid medications (e.g., amoxicillin, azithromycin, augmentin, insulin, pediatric suspensions) have concentrations such as:

250 mg / 5 mL

100 mg / 1 mL

1 g / 5 mL

Providers prescribe mg per dose, not mL.
Pharmacies dispense mL, not mg.

Today our system does NOT:

Parse concentration strings like “250 MG/5 ML”

Convert mg → mL properly

Calculate total volume needed across the full therapy

Detect concentration mismatches

Choose the correct NDC bottle size

Validate that the prescribed mg dose fits the concentration

Warn when concentration data is missing or incompatible

Instead, the system incorrectly uses:

dose × frequency × days


This only works for tablets but is clinically invalid for liquids.

Example reported by pharmacist:
Prescription: 400 mg, 3 times daily, 7 days
Product: 250 mg / 5 mL
Correct calculation:

400 mg ÷ 50 mg/mL = 8 mL per dose

8 mL × 3 = 24 mL/day

24 × 7 = 168 mL total
Our system currently cannot compute this.

This leads to unsafe outputs and incorrect NDC recommendations.

🎯 Why This Is a Critical Issue

Liquid antibiotics are among the most commonly dispensed medications

Pediatric dosing errors are a top pharmacy risk

Pharmacists explicitly flagged this as a major gap

Our NDC calculator is currently unusable for liquid medications

We cannot support pediatric prescriptions without this fix

Insurance and compliance require accurate and validated quantity calculations

This is a blocker for clinical correctness.

📌 Definition of Done (Problem-Side Only)

(Not the solution – only what must be addressed.)

A complete fix requires that the system is able to:

Correctly interpret concentrations (mg/mL) from FDA NDC data

Convert prescribed mg dose into mL per dose

Calculate total mL needed based on frequency and day supply

Validate prescribed dose against available concentration

Select appropriate bottle size(s) that meet or exceed total mL

Provide warnings for:

missing concentration

incompatible dose

unsafe or ambiguous conversions

Generate accurate explanations for all liquid calculations

Maintain backwards compatibility with solid dosage workflows
